U-Net Deep Convolutional Architecture

BrainTumorAI incorporates a medical U-Net convolutional neural network specifically structured for biomedical image segmentation. The architecture consists of a contracting path (encoder) that captures contextual hierarchical representations and an expansive path (decoder) that enables precise spatial localization.

Long-range skip connections concatenate high-resolution feature maps from the encoder directly to the decoder stages, preventing the loss of fine-grained anatomical edge boundaries during downsampling.

Tensor Preprocessing & Delineation Flow

1
Grayscale Conversion & Padding Input MRI scans are converted to single-channel luminance (L) mode. Bilinear interpolation scales the image with aspect-ratio preserving zero padding to exactly 256×256 pixels.
2
Normalization & Tensor Ingestion Pixel intensities [0, 255] are normalized to floating-point range [0.0, 1.0] and reshaped into a 4D batch tensor of shape (1, 256, 256, 1).
3
Calibrated Thresholding (0.40) Continuous probability maps generated by the final sigmoid activation layer are binarized using a decision threshold of 0.40, which optimizes boundary sensitivity for hyperintense lesions.
4
Alpha Composite Visualization Positive coordinates are rendered as an alpha-composited RGBA layer (rgba(255, 30, 60, 0.45)) overlaid directly onto the normalized base MRI canvas.

Medical & Ethical Responsibility Statement BrainTumorAI is strictly designed for investigative research, model verification, and educational visualization. The software outputs binary segmentation masks based on 2D pixel-level statistical affinities and does not differentiate tumor sub-classes (e.g. glioma, meningioma, pituitary) or volumetric depth. It is not an FDA-cleared diagnostic device and should never replace clinical judgment by certified medical professionals.
